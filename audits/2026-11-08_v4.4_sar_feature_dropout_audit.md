# v4.4 — SAR Feature Dropout Audit (Why models rely on GEM faults, not SAR)

**Date:** 2026-11-08 · **Timestamp:** 2026-11-08T21:00:00+08:00 · **Type:** Data / training-pipeline audit (read-only, no code changed)

## Question
Why do the production K=4 / K=3 models depend on GEM Global Active Faults–derived channels and not on ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 SAR?

## Evidence

### 1. Model feature lists and XGBoost gain share
| Model | n_feat | SAR features in model | SAR gain share | Fault-derived gain share (GEM density/dist, Ts, Td) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `seven_pass_east_kalimantan/k4_ensemble` | 21 | none | 0.000 | 0.587 |
| `seven_pass_east_kalimantan/k3_coal_ensemble` | 21 | none | 0.000 | 0.668 |
| `seven_pass_kelian_corridor/k4_ensemble` | 21 | none | 0.000 | 0.773 |
| `seven_pass_kalimantan_50m/k4_ensemble` | 56 | 6 | 0.026 | 0.128 |
| `seven_pass_kalimantan_50m/k3_coal_ensemble` | 56 | 6 | 0.043 | 0.016 |
| `seven_pass_kutai_basin_100m/k3_coal_ensemble` | 52 | 6 | 0.083 | 0.071 |

### 2. Root causes
1. **SAR never reaches the main datasets.** `scripts/run_seven_pass_training.py` builds `feature_cols` from the columns of `dataset.parquet`. Only `kalimantan`, `kutai_basin_100m`, `polygons/kelian_corridor_250m`, and the two `unlabeled_tile*` datasets have `sar_*` columns. `east_kalimantan` (35 cols), `borneo`, every `hotspot_*`, and the national/island datasets have none. The 21-channel cube (`build_east_kalimantan_250m.py`, `run_indonesia_hd_upgrade.py`, `run_indonesia_national_pipeline.py`) contains DEM, gravity, and GEM-fault channels only.
2. **Warm-start feature alignment removes SAR without warning.** `run_seven_pass_training.py` L706–716: when `prior_ensemble.feature_names_` exists, `feature_cols` is **replaced** by the prior model's list. Kelian corridor has 81 columns including 6 SAR, but it was warm-started from `seven_pass_east_kalimantan/models` (21 features), so SAR and about 60 other columns were dropped. Any lineage warm-started from East Kalimantan inherits the 21-feature schema permanently.
3. **The SAR channels are synthetic.** `scripts/harvest_kalimantan_multimodal.py::fetch_and_process_sar` (L278–318) builds γ0 HH/HV from DEM slope and roughness plus seeded Gaussian noise (`default_rng(42)`, `default_rng(101)`). No PALSAR-2 mosaic is read. HH/HV therefore duplicate `dem_slope`/`terrain_ruggedness_tri` plus noise. Even where SAR is present, trees prefer the cleaner DEM/fault originals, which explains the low SAR gain share. `sar_bedding_strike_deg` and `sar_glcm_anisotropy` are derived from these synthetic grids. This conflicts with the Provenance Honesty guardrail.
4. **GEM channels are dense and carry spatial structure.** `active_fault_density`, `dist_active_faults_m`, and the Mohr-Coulomb `slip/dilation_tendency` (all computed from GEM traces) are smooth regional fields that act as strong location proxies under sparse positive labels.

## Recommendations
- Ingest real ALOS-2 PALSAR-2 annual mosaics (JAXA, 25 m HH/HV, e.g. via GEE `JAXA/ALOS/PALSAR/YEARLY/SAR_EPOCH`) and replace `fetch_and_process_sar`, or emit `sensor_source: not_available` until real data exists.
- Add SAR channels to the 21-channel cube builders for East Kalimantan, Borneo, and the hotspot datasets.
- In warm-start alignment, log the dropped columns and either train fresh, or train a challenger with the union feature set when new channels are available.
- After ingestion, run drop-column/permutation importance with spatial CV to measure SAR's real contribution.
