## Summary

1. **The metal heads are not trained on what their names say.** In East Kalimantan the "porphyry Cu" head has 10 deposits, and one of them is copper. Eight are granite, feldspar and potash-rock occurrences.
2. **The strongest feature in the deployed metals model is a map of which active fault is nearest.** `dilation_tendency_Td` is piecewise constant over regions of about 3,000 km² and carries 36–38% of the gain in the porphyry and skarn heads.
3. **The top of the deployed coal map is ranked by a hand-written formula of slope and regional TPI, not by the model.** In the top 1% of cells the map correlates 0.986 with that formula and 0.056 with the model score.
4. **The pipeline has two inference paths that compute the uncertainty bounds differently.** This is why the lower bound is zero and every target comes from the fallback (v4.12 §5.1), and why the coal upper bound is below the estimate (v4.12 §5.2).
5. **The real cube has five problems that must be fixed before any retrain on it:** one feature built from the porphyry label points, coal labels laid out across strike instead of along it, "bare ground" optical layers that are 79% sea, a "mine" mask that is 98% forest loss, and a propensity model that predicts its own definition.
6. **The gravity chain is partly topography.** The Bouguer slab correction uses a 250 m DEM on a free-air grid that is much coarser, so short-wavelength "Bouguer" is inverted terrain (r = −0.77 at 10 km).
7. **The potential-field operators, terrain formulas, stress formulas, SAR calibration and nnPU loss are implemented correctly.** The problems are in what is fed to them and in how their outputs are named and combined.

Severity below: **High** changes what a map or a metric means; **Medium** is a real defect with limited reach; **Low** is naming, dead code or a latent bug.

## 1. Geology and labels

### 1.1 The "porphyry Cu" head is mostly rock-type occurrences (High)

`build_k4_deposit_targets` (`src/labels/borneo_labels.py:217-229`) puts into head 0 every GeoRIMA node that is copper, **or** any base metal, **or** has role `porphyry_intrusive`, **or**, inside the EK box only, has role `felsic_intrusive` / `potassic_host`.

| GeoRIMA nodes in head 0 | Borneo (53) | EK window (10) |
|---|---|---|
| Copper (`Tembaga`) | 13 | **1** |
| Lead, zinc (`Timbal`, `Seng`) | 14 | 1 |
| Mercury, antimony (`Air Raksa`, `Antimon`) | 6 | 0 |
| Diorite, granodiorite, granite | 15 | 3 |
| Potash-bearing rock, feldspar | 5 | 5 |

- The lithology rows are `INDUSTRIAL_LITHOLOGY` records (quarry and industrial-mineral occurrences), not mineral deposits.
- Mercury and antimony are low-temperature epithermal pathfinders. Lead and zinc in Kalimantan are mostly vein, skarn or replacement style.
- The EK-only rule means the head has a different definition inside and outside the window.
- The harness reports 10 porphyry deposits in EK and a median-cell AUC of 0.85–0.92 (v4.12 §2). That number measures how well the model finds mapped felsic intrusive occurrences.

### 1.2 The skarn and epithermal heads carry similar assumptions (Medium)

- **Skarn:** all 57 ESDM `Besi Primer` (primary iron) records are treated as contact skarn, plus 14 GeoRIMA marble occurrences (`MARBLE` / `Marmer`, 6 of them in EK). Marble is a possible skarn host, not a deposit.
- **Epithermal:** every `Emas Primer` (primary gold) record is treated as epithermal. Kalimantan primary gold also includes sediment-hosted and intrusion-related styles.
- **MRDS gold:** Busang is a positive. Its gold resource was fraudulent; whether the minor real mineralisation there justifies a positive should be a decision, not a default.

### 1.3 Two alluvial placers pass the placer guard (Medium; violates AGENTS §3.2)

- `filter_valid_ore_candidates` removes records whose `commodity_label` is exactly `"Emas Alluvial"`. For MRDS the loader sets `commodity_label` to the site name, so the test never matches.
- Head 2 then takes any MRDS record whose `code_list` contains `"AU"`. That admits **Riam Kanan River** (`PGE AU GEM_D SN`) and **Cempaka-Danau Seran Paleochannels** (`GEM_D AU PGE`), both alluvial diamond-gold-PGE workings in South Kalimantan.
- They are outside the EK window, so they affect the island-wide label set only.

### 1.4 Skarn cells are negatives for the porphyry head (Low)

`compute_asymmetric_pu_weights` gives weight 1 to every cell that is not positive for head k, including cells positive for another head, unless they fall in head k's own exclusion halo. Skarn forms at porphyry contacts, and the stacked design (head 0 reads the skarn score with a +1 monotone constraint) assumes the two go together.

### 1.5 Coal rank labels are laid out across strike, not along it (High)

- `_structure_tensor_eigen_and_strike` (`coal_and_multimodal_features.py:85-128`) returns θ with the strike vector `(row, col) = (cos θ, sin θ)`. θ = 0 means a ridge running north–south.
- `build_k3_coal_rank_targets` (`borneo_labels.py:1007-1010`) steps with `(row, col) = (sin θ, cos θ)`. θ = 0 steps east–west.
- The two agree only at θ = 45° and 135°. At 0° and 90° the halo is perpendicular to strike.
- **Check:** north–south stripes give strike 0° and a halo of 28 columns × 0 rows; east–west stripes give 90° and 28 rows × 0 columns.
- Each convention has its own passing unit test (`test_coal_and_multimodal_pipeline.py:161` states "0 = East-West along columns"). The mismatch only appears where `build_real_feature_cubes.py:524-526` passes one into the other.
- This adds to v4.12 §3.1: the generated coal positives are not only generated, they are in the wrong place.

## 2. Physics and feature engineering

### 2.1 `dilation_tendency_Td` and `slip_tendency_Ts` are nearest-fault zoning maps (High)

How they are built (`build_real_feature_cubes.py:199-212`):
1. `strike` at every cell = strike of the **nearest GEM active-fault segment**, at any distance.
2. Td and Ts are computed from that strike and an assumed stress tensor (σ1 = 95 MPa, σ3 = 25 MPa, azimuth 20°).
3. The result is multiplied by `0.35 + 0.65·exp(−d / 3.5 km)`, where d is `dist_active_faults_m` (see 2.2).

Measured:

| | Real EK cube | Old (deployed) EK cube |
|---|---|---|
| Distinct values of Td ÷ distance factor | **14** | 598 |
| Share of land in the 20 largest constant patches | 99.9% | 84.7% |
| Median area of those patches | 2,993 km² | 3,043 km² |

- Step 1 makes the field a Voronoi partition of the fault map. A cell 150 km from any fault inherits that fault's strike.
- In the deployed model Td has the largest gain share in three heads: porphyry 36%, skarn 38%, epithermal 17%. Ts adds 3–9%.
- v4.8 removed smooth regional fields from the metals heads because they act as coordinates. Td is the same kind of feature and was kept. v4.12 §5.5 notes that it is counted twice (model and ranking); it does not note that it is a location partition.
- **The stress tensor is assumed and uncited.** The NNE–SSW fold axes of the Samarinda anticlinorium imply shortening roughly WNW–ESE, about 90° from the assumed σ1 azimuth. I did not check this against the World Stress Map. The relevant stress for Miocene mineralisation is in any case the palaeo-stress, not today's.
- GEM lists **active** (Quaternary) faults. Borneo has few, and they are not the structures that controlled Miocene ore systems.

### 2.2 `dist_active_faults_m` is mostly a terrain-curvature distance (Medium)

- Definition: `min(distance to GEM fault, 2.5 × distance to "DEM lineament")`.
- "DEM lineament" = the top 14% of cells by smoothed |plan curvature|. There is no test for linearity; ridges and valleys of any shape qualify.
- On the real EK cube the second term is the minimum on **77.2%** of land (13.3% on the old cube). `active_fault_density` blends the same two sources 60/40.
- `dist_topo_lineaments_km` is therefore present twice, and the site cards file it under "crustal conduits".

### 2.3 The Bouguer anomaly contains inverted topography (Medium)

- `bouguer_slab_reduction` subtracts `0.1119 × h` using the 250 m DEM. The constant is correct for 2,670 kg/m³.
- The free-air grid is far coarser than the DEM. Over land the Sandwell & Smith product is not altimetry; it carries a global model (I did not verify its onshore data coverage for Borneo).
- Where the free-air grid has no signal at a given wavelength, the correction leaves `−0.1119 × h` behind.

| High-pass scale | Corr(Bouguer, DEM) | Regression slope (mGal/m) |
|---|---|---|
| σ = 30 km | −0.50 | −0.040 |
| σ = 10 km | −0.77 | −0.067 |

- The full slab term is −0.112, so at 10 km about 60% of it is uncompensated. The old cube gives the same figures (−0.48, −0.73).
- Consequences: `gravity_residual_30km_mgal` and `gravity_residual_basin_mgal` are partly terrain. AGENTS §4.3 says the DEM-proxy gravity was removed; at short wavelengths it is back.
- Fix: low-pass the DEM to the gravity grid's resolution before the slab correction.

### 2.4 Other gravity-chain items (Medium / Low)

- **`crustal_thickness_km` is the Bouguer field again** (r = −0.91). It is `32 − (band-limited Bouguer) / 16.77`, clipped.
- **"Parker–Oldenburg" is a label, not the method** (`geophysics.py:829-841`). The continuation depth is capped at 12 km instead of 32, the gain is capped at e^1.2, and there is one pass with no iteration. It is a low-pass filter with a slab conversion.
- **"Complete Bouguer"** in docstrings is a simple Bouguer anomaly; there is no terrain correction.
- **`gravity_residual_basin_mgal`** adds a modelled term, `−3.5 mGal × CRUST1.0 sediment km`, to the measured residual. The Bouguer field already contains the sediment effect, so this counts it twice. CRUST1.0 has about 30 nodes across the EK window.
- **`sediment_thickness_m`** is a 111 km product interpolated to 250 m. It is smooth enough to act as a coordinate.

### 2.5 Magnetics (Low)

- The RTP filter is correct (see §7). It uses one inclination (−14°) for an island that spans roughly −8° to −23°.
- EMAG2 covers 28.5% of EK land. The FFT input is zero-filled elsewhere, and at low latitude the RTP operator has a long north–south response, so values near coverage edges are contaminated. RTP has a median of −39 nT inside coverage although the input was demeaned.
- EMAG2 values are clean (−113 to +110 nT); the nodata handling works.

### 2.6 The "bare ground" optical layers are mostly water (High, real cube)

- The canopy gate keeps cells with NDVI < 0.20. Water has negative NDVI and passes. Sentinel-2 SCL class 6 (water) is kept as clear.
- Of the 174,924 real-EK rows with a valid `spec_clay_hydroxyl_gated`: **78.9% have elevation below 0 m and 90.6% have NDVI < 0.**
- So `spec_*_gated` and `aster_*_gated` are, for the most part, sea, river and pond pixels. To a tree model "this layer is not missing" means "this is water".
- Related: the training grid keeps cells down to −20 m. **6.0% of real-cube rows (5.5% of old-cube rows) are below sea level.** They sit in the training background and in the harness's ranking background, which makes every AUC slightly easier.

### 2.7 `active_mine_mask` is a forest-loss mask (High, real cube)

- Rule: `(NDVI < 0.18 and loss ≥ 0.45) or loss ≥ 0.75 or HH > −2.5 dB`.
- It covers **13.2% of EK land (27,900 km²)**. The `loss ≥ 0.75` clause alone gives 13.0%.
- Forest loss in Kalimantan is mostly plantation, logging and fire.
- **The radar clause points the wrong way.** L-band HH is −6.8 dB over forest and −13.4 dB over sparsely vegetated land in this cube. Bare ground is darker than forest, so `HH > −2.5 dB` selects 0.19% of land and none of it is bare. The same test is in the coal label rule (`borneo_labels.py:943`).
- The mask feeds the coal label rule, the AMD censoring and the propensity target.

### 2.8 The propensity model predicts its own definition (High, real cube; AGENTS §4.12 is false)

- **Target.** `explored_footprint` is 16.9% of land, and 77% of those cells are there because `forest loss ≥ 0.75` (`build_pooled_exploration_footprint`).
- **Covariates.** The orchestrator's list (`run_seven_pass_training.py:1178-1192`) includes `gfw_forest_disturbance` and `forest_disturbance_rate`. A logistic model on those two alone predicts the target with AUC 0.93.
- **The documented restriction is not applied.** `pu_negatives.py:526-544` defines the allowed columns (roads, rivers, ports) and a forbidden list (slope, elevation, DEM, haul roads, disturbance, outcrop, regolith). The orchestrator does not call that function. Seven of its ten covariates are on the forbidden list.
- In the deployed run the old cube had only the terrain covariates, so that propensity is a terrain model.

### 2.9 A real-cube feature is built from the porphyry label points (High, real cube)

- `dist_calc_alkaline_intrusive_km` is the distance to GeoRIMA nodes whose commodity or role names an igneous rock (`coal_and_multimodal_features.py:585-594`).
- Those include the diorite, granodiorite and granite nodes that are head-0 positives (1.1).
- On the real EK cube: 28% of porphyry-positive cells are within 1 km of such a node, against 0.1% of background. The feature alone gives a cell-level AUC of **0.879**.
- v4.12 recommendation 7 says to ablate the lithology distances. This one should simply be excluded while the labels include the same points.

### 2.10 Smaller physics items (Low)

- **Thermal anomaly.** A 6.5 K/km air lapse rate is applied to land surface temperature. The result tracks land cover weakly (+1.0 K where forest loss ≥ 0.75).
- **ASTER `L13/L10`.** This is a ratio of radiances 2.4 µm apart with no temperature–emissivity separation. By Planck's law it changes about 8% between 300 K and 320 K, comparable to a silica contrast. Its observed range on EK is 1.16–1.19, so it carries little. It is not one of Ninomiya's indices (those are QI, CI and MI); QI is implemented correctly.
- **`dem_twi`.** Flow accumulation runs for 32 steps (about 9 km) with no pit filling, so large rivers are underestimated. Mine "contamination" also stops after 32 steps.
- **Naming.** `slope` says Horn (1981) and uses central differences. `terrain_ruggedness_tri` is a 5×5 standard deviation. `dem_aspect` and `sar_bedding_strike_deg` are circular and used as raw degrees (noted in v4.10).

## 3. Models and calibration

### 3.1 Coal: the top of the map is the basin gate, and the gate is running on defaults (High)

The coal map is `gate × noisy-OR(min(1, raw_c / ĉ_c))` with ĉ on its 0.10 floor.

- Where any rank's raw score exceeds about 0.10 the noisy-OR saturates at 1 and the map equals the gate.
- `MacroBasinSequenceGate._get_col` returns a default when a column is absent. In the deployed feature list `sediment_thickness_m`, `gravity_residual_basin_mgal`, `dist_sedimentary_polygon_km`, `mag_analytic_signal` and `sar_moisture_index` are all absent.
- The gate therefore reduces to `0.777 + 0.15 × σ((25 − slope)/5) × σ((30 − TPI_regional)/25)`, range 0.777–0.926.

| Share of map | Cells saturated | Corr(map, gate) | Corr(map, model score) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Top 1% | 98.5% | **0.986** | 0.056 |
| Top 5% | 72.1% | 0.397 | 0.437 |
| Top 10% | 36.2% | 0.081 | 0.664 |

- The reported `p_coal_any_max` of 0.9255 is the gate's maximum.
- On the real cube the inputs exist, and 65% of the gate becomes CRUST1.0 sediment thickness (weights 0.40 and 0.25 both draw on it). The magnetic rule looks for `mag_analytic_signal`; the real cube calls it `emag2_analytic_signal`, so that rule stays dead.
- The optical "weight of evidence" stage (AGENTS §4.5) has no optical heads in the deployed model (`optical_heads_` is empty).

### 3.2 Root cause of the zero lower bound (High)

- **In-memory path** (`run_seven_pass_training.py:1549-1555`): calls `compute_multi_mineral_conformal_bounds` without `q_res_k`, which gives `L = p − 0.5σ − (1 − q_set)`.
- **Chunked path** (`:431-434`), used for any region over 150,000 rows: `L = p − q_res × σ`, where `q_res` is the 90th percentile of `(1 − p)/σ` over validation positives.
- σ has a floor of 0.01, so `q_res` is large (79 in a synthetic replay). `L` is then 0 unless `p` is near 1.
- On the deployed map `L > 0` on 0.00% of land. The 96th percentile of `L` is 0, the threshold clips to 0.35, nothing passes, and the fallback runs.
- The same model gives different bounds depending on region size. Neither formula has a coverage meaning: both are calibrated on positives only and applied as a two-sided band.

### 3.3 Root cause of the coal upper bound below the estimate (High)

- `upper_c = max over ranks of (cal_c + 0.5σ)` is computed **before** the hierarchical step.
- The published estimate is `gate × noisy-OR(cal_c)`, computed **after**.
- A noisy-OR of three ranks exceeds their maximum, so the estimate overtakes the bound (65% of cells).
- The conformal quantile is calibrated in memory on the post-hierarchical values and applied in the chunked path to the pre-hierarchical ones.

### 3.4 Why ĉ sits on its floor (Medium)

- Every head uses `max_delta_step = 1`, 100 trees and learning rate 0.05. The margin can move at most `100 × 0.05 × 1 = 5` from the base score.
- With a training prevalence near 0.6% the base logit is about −5.1, so the raw probability cannot exceed about 0.48. The deployed composite's maximum is 0.482.
- ĉ is the mean raw score over spatially held-out positives. It cannot approach the values Elkan–Noto assumes, and lands under 0.10 for every head.
- So the floor is a property of the training settings, not of the data. It adds to v4.10 finding 4.

### 3.5 The ensemble spread is not epistemic uncertainty (Medium)

- `_BaggedXGBoostHead.fit` keeps **all positives in every bag** and resamples only the background (`metals.py:337-340`).
- The spread across bags reflects background sampling and row subsampling. It does not reflect which deposits are known, which is the dominant uncertainty with 10–13 deposits per head.
- Every σ downstream (bounds, site cards, coal ranking) is too small. A bootstrap over deposit clusters would measure the right thing.

### 3.6 Prediction-area feature weights have a side effect (Medium)

- `compute_pa_intersection_weights` output is passed to XGBoost as `feature_weights`, which sets column-sampling probability (`colsample_bytree = 0.8`).
- Check on XGBoost 3.4.1: with 10 features, giving weight 0 to the two informative ones removes them from the model completely.
- A feature whose relation to deposits is not monotone gets a low weight and is under-sampled in every tree.
- The weights are computed on all training rows before the internal out-of-fold split, not per fold as the class docstring says.
- Several hand-picked "prior" feature names boosted by ×1.25 do not exist in either cube (`dem_twi` in the old cube, `in_ultramafic_belt`, `in_carbonate_host`, `dist_carbonate_km`).

### 3.7 Smaller model items (Medium / Low)

- **Composite map.** `max` over heads of scores on different scales. The skarn head has the most positive cells and supplies 64% of the top 1% of the metals composite.
- **Stacking skew.** Head 0 trains on out-of-fold scores from models with half the trees and one bag, and predicts with scores from the full models. v4.8 found stacking has no measurable effect.
- **Weighted conformal quantile** (`conformal.py:73-74`). The finite-sample correction is discarded by a condition that can never be true. With uniform weights and n = 20 it returns 0.863 where the unweighted branch returns 0.982.
- **Coal σ** uses `(1 − w)²σ_sub² + w²σ_opt²`, the variance of a convex blend. The fusion is additive in log-odds.
- **Coal `p_any`** is a noisy-OR over ranks that are mutually exclusive.
- **SHAP.** Computed on bag 0 only, so values do not sum to the reported score. Site cards take the feature vector of the nearest row in the 117,196-row sample, which is usually not the target's centroid cell.
- **Zonation features** (AGENTS §4.10). Inactive in production. If enabled they change formula silently above 50,000 rows and in the validation gate. The feature names in AGENTS do not exist in the code.
- **U-Net.** The native pixel size is passed for a grid decimated ×5. See v4.12 §4 for the rest.

## 4. Evaluation

- **The "unified buffered spatial CV" cannot be used with its defaults (Medium).** `spatial_buffered_block_kfold` uses 5 km blocks, random fold assignment and a 6 km buffer. On a 100 × 100 km grid it keeps 11–14% of cells for training (80% without the buffer). Nothing in production calls it (AGENTS §4.11 says all splitting was consolidated into it).
- **The in-script "leakage-free" CV** trains 30-tree, 2-bag models with weights and masks from the production fold. Its output is copied to `blind_holdout.json`. Already in v4.5 and v4.10; still true.
- **The validation fold is used four times:** warm-start gate, blend weights, ĉ, and conformal quantiles. Its PR-AUC is then reported.
- The deposit-level harness remains the only trustworthy measurement. Its findings are in v4.10 and v4.12.

## 5. Architecture and code execution

### 5.1 Structure

- `run_seven_pass_training.py` is 3,174 lines; `run_region_seven_pass` is about 2,200 of them. Metals and coal are near-duplicate branches.
- **Two scoring implementations** exist for the same model (in-memory and chunked). They have diverged in the bounds (3.2, 3.3) and in NaN handling. One scoring function used by both would remove this class of bug.
- **Features are chosen by exclusion.** Every column not on a deny-list becomes a feature. Adding a layer to a cube changes the model (v4.12 §2.2).
- K = 4 and K = 3 are hard-coded through the model, calibration and output code.
- Large parts of `src/` are not on the production path: buffered CV, zonation, Merdith contrasts, crustal worms, AEM, Cloude–Pottier, Freeman–Durden, stream-sediment unmixing, precision-weighted fusion, logit-scaling calibration, `FeedbackLoopError`. `verify_immutable_positive_lake` does not exist.

### 5.3 Dashboard (Medium, known and open)

- No authentication, `Access-Control-Allow-Origin: *`, and the POST handler parses JSON whatever the content type.
- Any web page open in a browser on this machine can POST to `/api/train`, `/api/train/stop`, `/api/runs/delete` or `/api/clean_old_training`. A server is listening on 127.0.0.1:8765 now.
- AGENTS §6.3 requires the open CORS header on every response.

### 5.4 Latent bugs in unused code (Low)

- `d8_upstream_labels`: `site_of[-1]` is read when a path leaves the grid, which is the last cell.
- `compute_merdith_contrasts`: the ratio is discontinuous at `p20 = 0` and mixes a dimensionless branch with a unit-dependent one.
- `compute_ultramafic_plateau_gate`: `np.percentile` on an array with NaN returns NaN.
- `precision_weighted_log_odds_fusion`: weights logits by the variance of probabilities.
- `calibrate_monotonic_logit_scaling`: uses ĉ (a label frequency) as if it were a class prior.

### 5.5 Tests

285 pass. They test functions in isolation. Two modules with opposite strike conventions both pass. There is no test that the two inference paths agree, and none that a label source is disjoint from a feature source.

## 6. The training process as it actually runs (deployed EK run, 2026-11-09)

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| Data | Old EK cube, 2,920,344 rows (5.5% below sea level). All 4,160 positive cells plus 80,000 random background rows = 117,196 rows. |
| Features | 27 by exclusion. Metals heads drop 7 regional fields and use 20; coal sub-canopy heads use 15. |
| Folds | 25 km blocks, random assignment, 5 folds, no buffer. One fold is chosen to hold the most heads with positives and is withheld: 90,842 rows train. |
| Propensity | Logistic regression on terrain covariates; target = footprint or any positive. |
| Weights | Positives 1/e(x) normalised to mean 1; background 1; exclusion halo of 3.5–10 km per head set to 0. |
| Metals | Three heads fitted directly, porphyry stacked on skarn and epithermal scores. Each head: 5 bags × 100 trees, depth 6, learning rate 0.05, `min_child_weight` 10, `max_delta_step` 1. No early stopping. |
| Coal | Three rank heads on generated halo cells, same tree settings at depth 5. No optical stage. |
| U-Net | Off. |
| Calibration | ĉ on the withheld fold: 0.10 (floor) for all metal heads; coal 0.10, 0.10, 0.123. Metals × PCA+GMM envelope gate; coal × basin gate. |
| Bounds | Chunked path. Lower bound 0 on all land. |
| Targets | Fallback: top 4% of the calibrated map, closing, polygons, 15 greenfield + 15 brownfield per domain. |
| Reported | Validation-fold PR-AUC 0.047 (metals), 0.026 (coal). Not a test metric. |
| Never done | Refit on all deposits; deposit-level evaluation of this exact configuration before deployment (done after, in v4.12: 0.844). |

Deposit-level performance is in v4.12 §2–3 and is not restated here. Read it with 1.1 in mind: the best head is the one whose labels are least like its name.

- **Plan curvature:** dimensionally consistent, sign matches the docstring (valleys negative), invariant to the row axis pointing south. **Aspect:** correct for north-up rasters.

- **nnPU loss:** matches Kiryo et al. (2017), including the gradient step when the negative risk is clamped.
- **Sentinel-2:** the −1000 DN offset for baseline ≥ 04.00, index formulas, dark-pixel mask. **Landsat:** ST scale and offset, QA bits. **ASTER:** unit conversion coefficients and band-13 constants. **PALSAR-2:** −83 dB calibration and averaging in linear power.
- **EMAG2 nodata, the provenance gate, symlink-safe archive copies.**
- **Bouguer slab constant** 0.1119 mGal/m.

## 9. Recommendations (in order)

**Before any retrain**
1. **Rebuild the metal labels from deposit records only.** Drop `INDUSTRIAL_LITHOLOGY` and `MARBLE` nodes and the EK-only rule; move Hg/Sb out of porphyry; filter MRDS placers by commodity code. Rename heads to what they hold if they stay broad ("Cu and base metals", "primary Au").
2. **Remove `dilation_tendency_Td` and `slip_tendency_Ts` from the metals features**, or rebuild them as NaN beyond a few km of a mapped fault. Test with the harness.
3. **Fix the real cube:** mask water out of the canopy gate and drop cells below 0 m from the grid; exclude `dist_calc_alkaline_intrusive_km`; correct the strike vector in `build_k3_coal_rank_targets`; drop the `HH > −2.5 dB` clause; stop calling forest loss a mine mask or an exploration footprint.
4. **Use the documented propensity function** with roads, rivers and ports only.

**Pipeline**
5. **One scoring function for both inference paths.** Add a test that they agree on a small region.
6. **Stop publishing bounds and "calibrated probabilities".** Publish area percentiles, which the harness measures (v4.10, v4.12 agree).
7. **Coal:** take the basin gate out of the published score, or make missing inputs an error.
8. **Fix the CLI:** real cubes as defaults, U-Net off, `--train-10x` either working or removed, an explicit feature list file read by both production and the harness.
9. **Bootstrap over deposits** if an uncertainty is wanted.

**Physics**
10. **Low-pass the DEM before the Bouguer correction;** drop `crustal_thickness_km`; rename the "Parker–Oldenburg" function.

**Housekeeping**
11. Add a token to the dashboard POST routes and stop sending open CORS on them.
12. Correct AGENTS.md §3–§5 against the table in §8.
13. Delete or quarantine unused modules so that tests and docs describe what runs.

## 10. Limits of this audit

- East Kalimantan cubes and the deployed EK run only. The island cube and all other runs were not opened.
- Cell-level single-feature AUCs (1.1, 2.9) are not deposit-level; they show the direction and size of a leak, not a harness result.
- The stress-direction and gravity-source statements in 2.1 and 2.3 rest on general knowledge and on the measured correlations; neither was checked against a primary source in this session.
- No training was run, so none of the recommendations has been tested.
