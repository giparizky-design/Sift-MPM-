# v4.6 — Does Training on a Larger Area Help? (Kalimantan-Wide vs East Kalimantan)

**Date:** 2026-11-09 · **Timestamp:** 2026-11-09T16:30:00+08:00 · **Type:** Training / evaluation experiment
**Code:** [`scripts/run_deposit_cv.py`](file:///Volumes/Seagate%202/Geospatials/scripts/run_deposit_cv.py) with the new `--features`, `--eval-bounds`, `--train-pos-bounds` and `--train-bg-bounds` options (CHANGELOG v4.5.1)
**Outputs:** [`outputs/deposit_cv/expand/`](file:///Volumes/Seagate%202/Geospatials/outputs/deposit_cv/expand/)

## Question
Would more labelled sites, and a larger extent with more barren or unlabeled ground, improve the metals model?

## Data inventory
- **Islands outside Kalimantan.** The Papua, Sumbawa, Sulawesi, Sumatra and Indonesia datasets have **no K=4 label columns**, so they cannot add positives. The orchestrator also enforces a Kalimantan-only training policy.
- **Kalimantan-wide dataset.** [`data/kalimantan/processed`](file:///Volumes/Seagate%202/Geospatials/data/kalimantan/processed/) is 250 m, 3600×4400 cells, 8.25M valid. Its grid is aligned with East Kalimantan (EK): a +520 row and +2400 col offset. It has **168 deposit clusters**: 44 porphyry, 60 skarn, 55 epithermal and 18 laterite. 33 lie in the EK window and **135 lie outside**. Labels agree exactly with EK in the overlap.

**Cause of the fault-channel mismatch.** Both builds start from the same GEM faults file.
- [`run_indonesia_hd_upgrade.py`](file:///Volumes/Seagate%202/Geospatials/scripts/run_indonesia_hd_upgrade.py) (L262-L284) blends GEM fault distance with 2.5× DEM topographic-lineament distance, mixes the densities 60/40, and decays proximity over 3.5 km.
- [`harvest_kalimantan_multimodal.py`](file:///Volumes/Seagate%202/Geospatials/scripts/harvest_kalimantan_multimodal.py) (L428-L442) uses GEM only, with a 45 km decay.

The EK layers match the first recipe. The script that wrote them is not recorded, so that attribution is inferred. Either way, the `dilation_tendency_Td` that dominated the v4.5 EK result is partly a terrain-lineament feature. The two datasets therefore cannot be mixed, and every condition below uses the **Kalimantan-wide dataset only**.

## Design
- **Features.** The 18 channels shared with the EK production schema, minus the synthetic SAR: fault (4), lineaments, gravity (2), crust, DEM (7), coast, regolith and exposure.
- **Folds.** The same 5 folds over the same **33 EK deposits** in every condition: 25 km blocks, **15 km buffer**.
- **Scoring.** Each deposit is ranked against background cells inside the EK window. Production settings (100 trees, 5 bags), 80k training background cells in every condition.
- **Conditions:**
  - **A, EK only:** positives and background from inside the EK window.
  - **B, EK positives + island background:** adds barren and unlabeled ground from the whole island. The 135 non-EK deposits and 15 km around them are excluded.
  - **C, whole island:** all non-held-out deposits island-wide, plus island background.
  - **D, island-wide CV:** C's training, but all 168 deposits are held out and scored against island-wide background.

## Findings
1. **More barren or unlabeled area does not help.** With the training background held at 80k cells, drawing it from the whole island instead of EK leaves XGBoost unchanged (+0.006, not significant) and lowers logistic regression slightly (−0.012).
2. **More deposits from elsewhere in Kalimantan do not help EK, and probably hurt it.**
   - Logistic regression gets clearly worse: −0.097, with 24 of 33 deposits ranked lower.
   - XGBoost drops 0.063, but its paired CI just includes zero.
   - Likely reasons: deposits elsewhere on the island have a different signature in these regional features; or labels outside EK are noisier. These data cannot separate the two.
3. **Expansion does help the heads that had no data.** Laterite goes from 1 deposit (not learnable) to 18, and island-wide CV scores it 0.95. Porphyry and skarn also get tight intervals with 44–60 deposits.
4. **Epithermal is the weakest head island-wide (0.72).** These regional inputs don't capture its controls.
5. **Calibration is unstable.** The OUTMOGC and Elkan-Noto step cuts AUC by 0.12–0.16 in A and B, but slightly helps in C. It fits too few training deposits to be reliable.
6. **Feature recipes differ between datasets.** The same feature name means different things in different datasets. Any pooling, transfer or model reuse across regions needs a single feature recipe first.

## Recommendations
1. Keep EK maps trained on EK for porphyry, skarn and epithermal. Use the Kalimantan-wide model, with island-wide CV, for laterite.
2. Before more pooling experiments, rebuild the fault, stress and SAR channels with **one** recipe across all regional datasets, and record provenance. Then rerun C against A.
3. If pooling is retried, test region-aware approaches (a model per province, or a shared model fine-tuned per region). Don't add coordinates, which leak location.
4. Make the training background size a tested setting rather than a fixed 80k, and keep it fixed across conditions when comparing extents.
