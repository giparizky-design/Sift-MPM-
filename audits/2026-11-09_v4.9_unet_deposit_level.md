## Question
Audit v4.8 turned the DAE U-Net off in production because it had never been validated at deposit level. Its 0.66 coal blend weight came from one validation fold, and the old coal map's top 1% lay 54 km from any coal label. Should the U-Net be re-enabled, for which domain, and at what weight?

## Verdict
**Keep the U-Net off in production, for both metals and coal. The recommended weight is `w_unet = 0`.**

- **The U-Net alone ranks held-out deposits far worse than XGBoost.**
  - Metals: any-metal AUC 0.823 vs 0.925, −0.102 [−0.135, −0.066]. It ranks only 2 of 33 deposits higher.
  - Coal: 0.669 vs 0.814, −0.145 [−0.169, −0.121]. It ranks 101 of 361 deposits higher.
- **No blend weight helps.** With the weight chosen on the training folds only, the blend is indistinguishable from XGBoost alone:
  - metals −0.002 [−0.008, +0.002];
  - coal +0.000 [−0.001, +0.001].
- **The production weighting rule loses AUC.** The rule sets the weight in proportion to pixel PR-AUC. Applied honestly (on held-out training folds), it gives:
  - metals −0.008 [−0.021, +0.003];
  - coal raw −0.008 [−0.013, −0.003], which is significant.
- **The old coal weight is harmful.** At w = 0.66, coal loses −0.019 [−0.030, −0.009], with significant drops on all three rank heads.

- **Median-cell scoring (v4.10) gives the same verdict, more strongly.**
  - For metals, the production rule loses −0.053 [−0.120, −0.002] and w = 0.66 loses −0.076 [−0.138, −0.025].
  - Epithermal is the only head where the U-Net points the other way (n.s., 11 deposits). The hint disappears at 15 epochs.
- **Training longer does not rescue it.** With the production `--train-10x` setting of 15 epochs, the U-Net overfits:
  - alone: metals −0.207, coal −0.228;
  - w = 0.10 already costs metals −0.030 [−0.059, −0.005].

Under the v4.8 acceptance rule (no significant paired drop in "any" AUC), re-enabling at the selected weights would pass, but only because those weights are about 0. They change nothing. Re-enabling would add runtime and an unvalidated component for no measured benefit.

## How it was measured
- **Harness and settings.** The v4.8 harness with a **15 km buffer**, 25 km blocks, 5 folds, seed 42, 100 trees and 5 bags. XGBoost uses the v4.8 adopted settings:
  - metals: drop the 7 `METALS_REGIONAL_PROXY_FEATURES`, `--weight-scheme hajek_neutral --gate standardized --min-child-weight 10`;
  - coal: `--coal-disk-mode exclude --coal-buffer-m 3500 --weight-scheme hajek_neutral` (depth 5).
- **Baselines.** The baselines are v4.8 runs `metals_M13_combo` and `coal_C4_all`. The new runs hold out the same folds and rank against the same background sample.
- **Sanity check.** The XGBoost trained alongside the U-Net (`unet/xgb_*`) reproduces both baselines exactly: ΔAUC 0.000 and 0 deposits changed, on every head.
- **U-Net adapter (`--models unet`).** For each fold, one network is trained from scratch on that fold's training cells only. Held-out deposits, every cell within 15 km of them, and withheld deposits are excluded from both its labels and its unlabeled pool. Everything else mirrors production Pass 3A.2 / 3B.2 and `MPMDistrictDAEUNet.cross_validate_spatial_blocks`:
  - input cube: feature-stack channels in stack order, distances capped at 15 km, z-scored on training cells;
  - decimation: the production rule, factor 5;
  - loss: nnPU (π = 0.05) plus 0.5 × reconstruction MSE, with 1/e(x) weights from the fold's propensity model;
  - subsample: ≤ 1,500 positive windows and 2× unlabeled windows (200 to 3,000);
  - training: 32-pixel windows, batch 64, AdamW at 1e-4, **3 epochs** (the production default);
  - inference: dense prediction on the coarse grid, projected back bilinearly.
- **Blend.** `(1 − w)·s_xgb + w·s_unet` on the uncalibrated head scores, clipped to [0, 1] as `blend_predictions_by_pr_auc` does. The result then gets the same calibration as the XGBoost-only variant: Elkan-Noto plus OUTMOGC gate for metals, and the ensemble calibration plus basin gate for coal. At w = 0 this reproduces XGBoost exactly (unit-tested).
- **Choosing w on the training folds only** (nested). For outer fold f, the XGBoost and U-Net are refit on each of the other four folds, with the remaining folds as training and both held-out zones buffered out. That is 20 extra fits per domain. The inner held-out deposits are ranked against 100k background cells outside fold f's zone. Two rules are applied to these inner predictions:
  - **srauc:** grid w ∈ {0, 0.1, …, 1}, maximising the inner success-rate AUC of the primary "any" score (metals calibrated, coal gated). Ties go to the smaller w.
  - **prauc:** the production rule. Per head, w_unet = AP_unet / (AP_xgb + AP_unet), using pixel PR-AUC on the inner held-out cells. A head with no inner positives gets the production fallback (metals 0, coal 0.25).
- **Fixed weights** are also reported: metals 0.30 (the orchestrator's fallback) and coal 0.25; 0.66 (the old coal weight) for both domains.
- **Device.** The U-Net ran on MPS (`SIFT_DEVICE=mps`), at about 20 s per fit. Production defaults to CPU, with the same network and loss.

- **Novelty.** The U-Net's top 1% sits near nothing: 4.6× near training labels and 0.8× near held-out deposits (5 km), against XGBoost's 16.9× and 10.6×. Its map is spread out, but it does not find the unseen deposits. The blends leave XGBoost's novelty unchanged.

## Median-cell scoring (free of the max-over-cluster bias, audit v4.10 finding 1)
Audit v4.10 found that scoring a deposit by the maximum over its cells inflates the null to about 0.65 for metals. That inflation is larger for rough maps. The U-Net map is smooth: 1.39 km cells upsampled bilinearly. Max-cell scoring could therefore understate the U-Net. The same comparisons using each deposit's median cell (`compare_deposit_cv.py --metric median_cell`; files `compare_*_median_cell.json`):

- **The verdict holds, and is stronger, without the bias.** For metals, the production rule and w = 0.66 become significant drops. Porphyry loses −0.14 at w = 0.66.
- **Epithermal is again the exception.** On median cells, the U-Net alone gives epithermal 0.681 against XGBoost's 0.624 (+0.057 [−0.141, +0.271], 6/5). The selected blend gives +0.032 [+0.000, +0.091].
  - Audit v4.10 finding 2 shows that epithermal lost about 0.06–0.10 median-cell AUC under v4.8.
  - The U-Net may carry part of what the dropped regional fields gave that head. Its input cube also lacks those fields, but its 44 km windows see regional context.
  - With 11 deposits this is not evidence for an epithermal-only U-Net weight, and the hint does not survive 15 epochs (next section).

## Sensitivity: 15 epochs (production `--train-10x`)
This run used no inner selection and fixed weights only (`run_v49_e15.sh`, about 110 s per fit). It checks whether the 3-epoch U-Net was simply undertrained. **More training makes it worse.**

|---|---|---|---|---|
| U-Net alone | 0.718, **−0.207 [−0.274, −0.143]** | 0.575, **−0.277** | 0.586, **−0.228 [−0.260, −0.195]** | 0.569, **−0.200** |
| w = 0.10 | **−0.030 [−0.059, −0.005]** | **−0.056** | −0.002 [−0.008, +0.003] | −0.005 [−0.013, +0.002] |
| w = 0.20 | **−0.045** | **−0.075** | **−0.020** | **−0.022** |

- **The 15-epoch network overfits.** It sees about 450 windows per fit, through a 64→1024-channel U-Net with a reconstruction decoder.
- **Any non-trivial weight now costs significant AUC** for metals, and from w = 0.2 for coal.
- **The 3-epoch epithermal hint does not survive.** At 15 epochs, epithermal falls to 0.615 (U-Net alone, max-cell) and every blend loses epithermal AUC. The hint is treated as noise.
- **Production `--train-10x` is worse than the default.** A run with the U-Net on and `--train-10x` (15 epochs) would be worse still.

## Why the U-Net fails here (code findings)
1. **Very few positive examples.**
   - `cross_validate_spatial_blocks` hard-codes `native_res_m=50`. The production factor of 5 therefore gives 1.39 km cells on the 278 m grid.
   - Labels are decimated with nearest-neighbour sampling. That leaves about one coarse positive per deposit: 31–64 positive windows per fit, against 450–583 native positive cells per head.
   - With the production unlabeled rule (2 × max(n_pos, 200)), each network sees about 450 windows over 3 epochs.
   - XGBoost trains on about 1,400 positive cells and 80k background cells.
2. **The ASPP is not resolution-adaptive at this grid.**
   - The dilation is `max(1, round(r / (16·px_size_m)))`, and the orchestrator passes the native 278 m, not the 1.39 km coarse pixel. Either way, every dilation rounds to 1.
   - The fourth radius (6 km) is computed but never used: only `b1`–`b3` are dilated.
   - AGENTS.md §4.7 ("dilations dynamically scaled by physical pixel size") is therefore inert here.
3. **The production blend weight is not tested at deposit level.**
   - The weight rule is proportional to pixel PR-AUC. A model with a quarter of XGBoost's AP still gets about 20% of the weight.
   - In production, the U-Net's scores are block-OOF over the orchestrator's folds, with no deposit buffer. The weight is then fitted on a single validation fold.
   - In the archived pre-v4.8 run (`run_20261109_121603_135270`), that rule gave a mean coal weight of 0.66. Nested and buffered, the same rule gives 0.19–0.51, and those weights still lose AUC.
4. **Other drift found:**
   - `get_best_device()` defaults to CPU on macOS. Its docstring says "CUDA → CPU / MPS", but MPS is used only via `SIFT_DEVICE`.
   - The orchestrator caps distance channels by index into `feature_cols`, not by name. This is harmless when the stack order equals the feature order, which is true for the current lists.

## Operational note (OpenMP)
Loading torch and XGBoost in one process breaks on this machine:
- With XGBoost first, the first CPU torch op deadlocks in a libomp barrier.
- With torch first, a multithreaded XGBoost fit segfaults (exit 139).

The orchestrator avoids both by setting `OMP_NUM_THREADS=1` and importing torch first. The harness now does the same, but only when `unet` is requested. As a result, XGBoost is single-threaded in U-Net runs, and the results are identical (sanity check above).

## Caveats
- **Only the production U-Net was tested.** This is its architecture, decimation, sampling and training budget, plus the 15-epoch variant above. A redesigned network might do better: native-resolution labels, disk-aware positive sampling, more epochs, and an ASPP that is actually multi-scale. That would be a new model, and it needs its own deposit-level test before any production use.
- **Seeds and device.** U-Net training is seeded (torch and numpy), but MPS kernels are not guaranteed deterministic. Repeat runs can differ slightly.
- **Small samples.** Metals has 33 deposits (10/13/11/1 per head), and laterite is `insufficient_data`. The epithermal hint is within noise.
- **The metals feature set is not the deployed one.** This test uses the 14 features of the evaluated v4.8 M13 configuration. Audit v4.10 finding 3 shows that the deployed `k4_ensemble.joblib` uses 20: those 14 plus 6 PALSAR-2 channels. Neither the 20-feature XGBoost nor a U-Net on those channels has been scored at deposit level.
- **Calibration differs from production.** Both arms use the ensemble's in-sample ĉ, not production's validation-fold ĉ, as in v4.5–v4.8. The comparison is like-for-like, but the absolute calibrated numbers differ from production.

## Recommendations
1. **Keep `--disable-unet` in production for East Kalimantan, both domains.** No production defaults were changed and production was not rerun.
2. If the U-Net is kept in the code, change the fallback blend weights to 0. Also replace the PR-AUC-proportional rule with a deposit-level, nested selection (as here), or drop the blend altogether. This needs the owner's approval.
3. Before any future U-Net work:
   - fix `native_res_m` so the decimation follows the real pixel size;
   - sample all positive cells of each deposit, not about one;
   - make the ASPP branches actually differ;
   - retest with this harness (`--models unet`).
